The Residential Canon

Real Houses.
Enduring Ideas.

Exceptional residential architecture is not defined by price or excess. It is defined by a clear idea, disciplined execution, and a relationship between structure, setting, and daily life.

This collection focuses on documented residences of lasting architectural significance. No speculative valuations. No invented listings. Each profile links to an official institution or recognized preservation source for further reading and visitor information.

Photography is used as editorial atmosphere; linked sources provide the authoritative record and property-specific imagery.

01

Organic Architecture

Fallingwater

Frank Lloyd Wright · Mill Run, Pennsylvania · 1935

Designed as a weekend home for the Kaufmann family, Fallingwater places cantilevered concrete terraces directly above Bear Run. Its power comes from making architecture and landscape feel inseparable.

The lesson: Design from the site outward. The setting should shape the plan, materials, views, and movement through the home.

02

Mid-Century Modernism

The Glass House

Philip Johnson · New Canaan, Connecticut · 1949

The transparent pavilion is the centerpiece of a 49-acre National Trust Historic Site containing fourteen structures. Its radical simplicity turns landscape, proportion, and carefully placed objects into the interior.

The lesson: Fewer elements demand better decisions. Restraint makes proportion, placement, and material quality impossible to hide.

National Trust site →
03

International Style

Villa Savoye

Le Corbusier & Pierre Jeanneret · Poissy, France · 1928–1931

Villa Savoye gave built form to Le Corbusier’s five points of modern architecture, including pilotis, a free plan, ribbon windows, a free façade, and a roof garden.

The lesson: Circulation is architecture. A great home is understood gradually through arrival, movement, framed views, and changing light.

Official monument →
04

Case Study House

Stahl House

Pierre Koenig · Los Angeles, California · 1960

Case Study House No. 22 used a glass-and-steel structure to turn a difficult Hollywood Hills site into one of postwar Los Angeles architecture’s defining images.

The lesson: A constraint can become the concept. The steep site, exposed structure, and panoramic view are not problems concealed; they are the house.

LA Conservancy →
05

Life, Work & Design

Eames House

Charles & Ray Eames · Pacific Palisades, California · 1949

The Eames House—Case Study House No. 8—combined an economical prefabricated structure with a richly personal interior. Preserved much as the designers lived in it, the home rejects the idea that modernism must feel sterile.

The lesson: Architecture supplies the framework; living supplies the character. Personal collections and useful objects can deepen a disciplined space.

06

The Principle

What Endures

A Fine Living Guide Standard

The most influential residences vary radically in appearance, yet share discipline: a strong response to place, honest materials, controlled light, purposeful circulation, and rooms designed around the rituals of actual life.

The lesson: Borrow principles, not appearances. A home becomes lasting when every decision supports the same clear idea.
